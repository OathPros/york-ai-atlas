INDEPENDENT PUBLIC-WEB CONTENT AUDIT / 2026-11-15 / NOT AN OFFICIAL YORK PUBLICATION

One front door for AI at York.
One maintained answer per question.

Recommendation: evolve the existing AI @ York into a task-based, searchable front door with shared summaries, a tool register and a policy register. Keep formal policies, specialist guidance, research sites, programme applications and protected learning systems with their accountable owners. Consolidate common answers and navigation, not every piece of specialist content.

Evidence base: 246 URL retrievals; 234 grouped inventory records across 18 York hostnames and 22 inferred publishing families. 69 resources have analyst-written summaries/actions; 165 supporting records are extracted leads needing triage. 36 PDF texts were extracted. The bounded link graph contains 667 relationships. 31 discovered URLs remain unreviewed.

Coverage limit: this is not a census of all York AI information. No complete campus-by-campus/French-language audit, authenticated systems, analytics, embedded videos, live tool configuration or external policy review was available. Retrieval failures and inferred ownership are labelled. Recommendations need owner approval.

What matters most

Do not migrate conflicting rules into a larger audience. The central student page frames documented AI use as allowed unless prohibited; LA&PS and Library guidance use an instructor-authorisation framing. A reachable older DRAFT-named operational guideline is still linked alongside a newer approved/revised edition. Ask the appropriate academic and administrative policy owners to resolve these first. This audit does not determine permission or supersession.

The present hub offers three branches, but users must navigate multiple teaching, research, Library and IT layers to answer ordinary questions. The same territory is repeatedly introduced, while specialist resources are often valuable and should remain. News, marketing, aliases, pointers, downloadable formats and independent rule summaries require different consolidation treatments.

Catalogue of need

Organise the future hub around the specific questions people bring, not around existing page titles. The catalogue contains 1047 atomic questions under 105 parent jobs, drawn from full-text mining of 164 York resources, 136 journey-walk questions checked by York-wide search, and 188 peer-university benchmark items. Every York answer is backed by a verbatim quote (1565 need-to-content mappings across 211 resources).

Three-phase delivery approach

1. Correct: confirm policy interpretation and document supersession; review data/retention claims; repair intended-destination failures; assign actual owners and establish task-success baselines.

2. Consolidate: publish the seven hub sections and common records; reuse approved rules; syndicate resource, training and news records; retain useful faculty, research and admissions journeys; preserve accessible and French-language equivalents.

3. Retire and measure: approve redirects only for truly superseded equivalents; preserve archives; monitor link errors and findability; measure successful completion of course-permission, tool-selection, citation, research and training tasks rather than claiming success from fewer pages.

Governance that prevents re-fragmentation

Appoint a hub product owner and a publisher for each family. Track canonical URL, user question, audience, task/topic, content type, owner, authority/scope, access, language and review date. Tool records additionally need product/licence, authentication, purpose, data classification and configuration limits. Use shared structured content, not copied paragraphs with a shared visual theme.

Suggested maintenance: monthly link checks; quarterly core-answer and tool reviews; event-driven updates after policy/licence changes; semiannual programme and research-directory checks. Confirm the cadence with responsible teams. Publish ownership, review date and an issue-reporting route.

Method and handoff

Discovery combined official-site search, a topic-filtered depth-two crawl from the hub/branch seeds, and targeted faculty/Library/research follow-up. The initial crawl stopped at 150 URLs; follow-up brought retrievals to 246. Requests were sequential with a 400 ms pause. HTML main content and Library navigation were extracted; PDFs were text-extracted. SAVY content was obtained through an alternate web fetch after the collector transport failed.

Two pairs of workshop PDFs have identical whitespace-normalised extracted text (AI Bias and AI Workflows in February/September upload paths). That does not prove binary/layout identity. Other clusters represent analyst-assessed overlap or conflict, not a measured duplicate percentage. Alias grouping uses final response URLs. HTTP 500 and transport failures are not treated as proof of sign-in restrictions or permanent outages.

Next evidence needed for a complete institutional migration: CMS exports, additional sitemap coverage, protected-content inventories supplied by owners, French/campus parity review, analytics/search logs, user task testing and owner sign-off of canonical destinations and governance.
